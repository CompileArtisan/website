* Prelude
What follows isn't a tech blog, but a very abstract story I wrote. I was briefly
inspired by [[https://protesilaos.com/][Protesilaos]] and his work. It's very abstract and perhaps illogical
too, but bare with me :)

* Climbing up the lighthouse
We're all in a journey to keep rising higher, the skies and the heaven beyond.
The height you're at could be a measure of success, a measure of time, or maybe
even a measure of happiness. Different people have different ways to climb up
this /lighthouse of heights/, and they can choose how much of it they want to
reveal to everyone else. Some climb up the staircase, while the others might fly
with their wings. Some ascend covertly from inside the lighthouse, while others
scale the outer walls for all to see. Some may keep all the windows shut tight
keeping the lighthouse insulated from the rest of the world, while the others
might want the windows wide open, for the whole world to know. You are your mind
and your emotions, while the lighthouse symbolizes your physical self.

One particular boy ascends heights using the staircase, although quite
erratically. He started with an environment where he was naturally more capable
than everyone to rise. He looks outside his window to interact with the rest of
the world and at this point of time, everyone outside his window looked upto
him. The boy kept climbing up the stair case till one day the lighthouse moved
somewhere else. The people and the surroundings around him changed. Everyone
outside now look down from windows above his own, and the world that had seemed
far below, had risen past him. He began to resent every step, and tried to jump
past them to leap forward and catch up, while everyone outside seemed to fly
high effortlessly. From such heights, the boy could no longer tell what object
fell from above by chance, and what was thrown at him with intent. Every thing
felt like an attack through his window.

But the boy kept rising and finally got back into a good pace, and people are
back to looking up to him. The staircase that once felt like a burden, became
his strength. 

* Windows shut?
The boy could scale heights of success, time and happiness through loads of
effort in climbing up the staircase of the lighthouse. The boy sees structure,
climbs systematically and values patterns and efforts. His own path, effort and
direction was his greatest asset and proof of purpose, and he felt closing his
windows actively fueling it. Everyone outside looking through his window were
attracted to the staircase, and not the boy himself. So the fix is simple: if
the staircase was the only thing people saw through the windows, keep the
windows closed, but let everyone use the staircase. He carried burdens, solved
problems and sacrificed his own comfort to create better outcomes for others,
but reducing everyone outside the lighthouse to mere shadows is the final form
things took after all that happened.

* Shadow
The boy completely disregarded any depth because it is only a shadow cast by his
friends is he chose to see. Some shadows climb up the stairs steadily, while
some shadows jump up the lighthouse. Among countless passing silhouettes, one
such shadow appeared to stay adrift. This one shadow floats and wanders, but
often spends time near windows. The shadow seemed to move wherever the wind took
it. When the wind grew stronger, the shadow danced around. While other shadows
climbed with purpose, this shadow wandered. This shadow wasn't determined to
chase heights at all. Instead, it drifted besides everyone's window. The boy
paid attention to this shadow, as it would wait at any open window and receives
whatever comes through it. It could be joy, laughter, anger or pain. The shadow
unfolds itself and welcomed all of it. One day this shadow stood beside the
boy's window too. The shadow kept returning to the boy's window. The boy began
talking to the shadow everyday. The shadow gained so much of trust that it
outweighed the boy's caution. The boy finally decided to open the windows. It
was then the boy realized that this shadow, was a butterfly.


* Structure and Color
The colors on her wings told stories. Not of where she had flown, but of every
window where she had stopped. Some colors had faded. Some patterns no longer
matched. Yet she still spread her wings as though they were whole. These marks
only stuck with her forever because a butterfly's wings don't heal. Once
damaged, they remain damaged. The butterfly never spread her wings to be
admired. She spread them because someone else needed somewhere soft for their
heart to land. Every window she visited left something behind. She accepted each
color exactly as it was. The boy never used the window to share his feelings.
The moment he opened them, he had all the time in the world to appreciate the
butterfly's wings.
